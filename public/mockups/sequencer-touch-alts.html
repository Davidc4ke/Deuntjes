The compose step, three ways

Streamlined touch sequencers for a dungeon turn. Big circular steps; tap to carve, tap again to retune or remove. New steps remember the last note you chose. Blips are placeholder sounds — the real thing keeps the full synth engine.

A

The Pad Altar

A drum-machine altar: one bar of 16 fat pads per page, swipe or arrows for the next bar. The note ribbon under your thumb retunes the selected pad and stays sticky for the next one. Other tracks appear as tiny ghost beads on each pad.

Sparse Soul — use at most 4 notes.
+ fastest carving; everything within thumb reach; pads big enough to never miss.
− melody reads left-to-right in rows of four — less "melodic" at a glance than a lane.
B

The Ritual Ring

One bar as a summoning circle: sixteen steps around the ring, a blood hand sweeps as it plays. The heart of the circle shows your current note — raise or lower it with the talons. Other tracks orbit as inner ghost rings.

Minor Key — only notes from a minor scale.
Bar I of II
+ the loop is literally a loop — rhythm placement is instant to read; pure dungeon theatre.
− pitch lives in the middle, so melodies take more taps; ring gets tight beyond 16 steps.
C

The Bead Ladder

Time flows down a chain of beads you scroll with one thumb, like the tracker — but each step is one huge bead. Tap a bead to carve it; tap it again and a note fan unfolds beside your thumb. Other tracks hang as slim ghost beads on the left.

Off the Grid — no notes on the downbeat.
+ keeps the current tracker mental model; infinite scroll; the note fan lands under the thumb.
− only ~8 steps visible at once; ghost beads are read, not edited, until you swap tracks.
